## Low-dose polylactic acid microplastics reduce methane emissions without increasing grain arsenic and cadmium risks in ratoon rice system

**Authors:** Sha Zhang, Qianrui Huangfu, Lu Wang, Zheng Chen, Dong Zhu

**Journal:** Journal of Environmental Sciences, 2026, article 105840

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jes.2026.105840

**Full text:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S100107422600745X

**ISSN:** 1001-0742

### Abstract

Biodegradable microplastics are accumulating in agricultural soils, with uncertain consequences for global methane (CH₄) budgets and food safety. We examined how a low, environmentally realistic dose (0.01 %, W/W) of polylactic acid (PLA) microplastics affects CH₄ and metal(loid) dynamics in an arsenic (As)-contaminated paddy over a 167-day semi-perennial (main plus ratoon) rice life cycle. The results showed that PLA amendment reduced cumulative CH₄ emissions by 36.4 % (from 8418 ± 538 to 5354 ± 270 mg/m²; p < 0.05) without affecting net CO₂ exchange. Significant flux difference emerged approximately 40 days post-transplanting, identifying a clear seasonal pattern. Concurrent in situ porewater monitoring (at both diel and seasonal scales) and process-informed modeling were consistent with a mechanism whereby PLA microplastics delayed or inhibited soil reduction. Although PLA altered porewater redox proxies (e.g., dissolved iron and As), grain As and cadmium (Cd) concentrations remained statistically indistinguishable from controls (all p > 0.2). Grain As (327–832 µg/kg) consistently exceeded the Chinese food standard across all treatments in the main season, reflecting inherent pedogenic contamination, whereas ratoon-season grain Cd fell significantly below it through seasonal physiological regulation, neither being a microplastic-induced effect. These results demonstrate that low-dose PLA microplastics can significantly reduce CH₄ emissions in flooded rice paddies without increasing grain As and Cd safety risks, although the underlying mechanisms remain to be fully resolved.

### Keywords

Biodegradable microplastics; Methane emissions; Soil redox dynamics; Heatwave; Toxic trace metal(loid); Ratoon rice
